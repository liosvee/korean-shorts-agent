# Bilingual original script and storyboard

Create script.ko.txt, script.en.txt, storyboard.json, claims.json and packaging.md
inside the project. Use templates/storyboard.json and templates/claims.json.

- One clear promise, a compelling factual hook, concise development, and a
  satisfying answer. Curiosity must be paid off; no misleading claims.
- Natural spoken Korean with an accurate English translation. Avoid unnatural
  literal translations, unsupported superlatives and culturally stereotyped copy.
- A fresh angle and examples distinct from reference videos. Document the
  originality differences in reference-analysis.md.
- A light next-video invitation only when it fits the subject; do not withhold
  the promised answer to force another watch.
- Verify every substantive factual assertion and mark uncertainty explicitly.
  Remove unsupported claims before approval. High-stakes topics need appropriate
  primary sourcing and careful scope.
- Plan 30–60 seconds initially. Label duration as estimated until narration is
  measured. Do not infer Korean speaking time from English word count.
- Specify new/generated/licensed assets, provenance and intended use per scene.
- Provide Korean title, English title, Korean description and relevant tags;
  avoid claims that packaging guarantees a particular click rate.

Present the full Korean script, English translation, storyboard and factual
sources. Inspect the actual available voices/models. Prepare the exact paid
plans and source-dated total estimate, then ask for approval. Do not generate
paid narration merely to estimate its duration. Reuse existing approval if it
clearly covers the final content and cost.
